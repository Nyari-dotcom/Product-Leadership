## 1. The strategic bet

**We bet redesigning the first-value experience will increase meaningful action within the first 7 days for new users who show early signs of declining wellbeing, creating a stronger foundation for continued engagement beyond the initial 90–120-day acute-use period.**

The current problem is not simply acquisition or initial conversion. Fable's most engaged users often get significant value from the product during a period of acute stress, resolve that need, and then stop using Fable.

The strategic opportunity is therefore to make the first experience strong enough to establish Fable as an ongoing mental-wellness companion rather than a product users only need during a difficult period.

### Mechanism

> **Better first-value experience → more meaningful action → stronger perceived value → continued engagement → improved post-90-day retention → higher LTV.**

### Primary product outcome

**60% of users who identify a meaningful decline take at least one recommended action within 7 days.**

This is the leading indicator that users are experiencing Fable's value early.

---

# 2. The assumptions

The business case rests on four critical assumptions:

### Assumption 1 — The redesigned experience changes behaviour

Redesigning the first-value experience will increase the proportion of users who take a meaningful recommended action within seven days.

### Assumption 2 — Early meaningful action predicts longer-term value

Users who take meaningful action early are more likely to continue using Fable beyond the initial acute phase.

### Assumption 3 — Continued engagement improves commercial retention

Users who continue engaging beyond 90 days are more likely to remain paying customers, increasing customer lifetime value.

### Assumption 4 — The economics remain viable under stress

The initiative remains financially attractive even if churn is materially higher or acquisition costs increase.

### Load-bearing assumption

**The single most important assumption is that increasing meaningful early action ultimately improves post-90-day retention.**

If meaningful action increases but users still leave once their acute need is resolved, the initiative improves engagement without solving Fable's underlying business problem.

Therefore, the experiment must measure both:

* **Leading indicator:** meaningful action within 7 days
* **Business outcome:** 90-day+ retention and LTV

---

# 3. Expected return

## Customer-level economics

The planning assumptions are:

| Metric                      | Planning assumption |
| --------------------------- | ------------------: |
| Monthly subscription        |             **$12** |
| Gross margin                |             **75%** |
| Gross profit/customer/month |              **$9** |
| Current paid monthly churn  |              **6%** |
| CAC                         |             **$18** |
| Minimum LTV:CAC             |             **3:1** |
| Maximum acceptable payback  |        **6 months** |

### Base-case economics

At 6% monthly churn:

**LTV = $9 ÷ 6% = $150**

**LTV:CAC = $150 ÷ $18 = 8.3:1**

**CAC payback = $18 ÷ $9 = 2 months**

This establishes the economic baseline against which the retention initiative is assessed.

---

# 4. Product impact at scale

Assume Fable has **3,000 weekly active users** entering the relevant experience.

If 60% take a meaningful action:

**3,000 × 60% = 1,800 users taking meaningful action per week.**

However, these users should **not** be treated as 1,800 new paying customers.

The commercial value comes from the proportion of these users who subsequently:

1. remain engaged beyond the initial acute period;
2. remain subscribed; and
3. generate sufficient lifetime gross profit to recover CAC and produce the required return.

The experiment therefore needs to establish the relationship between:

**Meaningful action → 90-day retention → paid retention → LTV.**

---

# 5. Retention sensitivity

Fable's central business risk is retention.

The base case assumes:

**6% monthly churn**

We stress-test the model by increasing churn by **10 percentage points**:

**6% → 16% monthly churn**

At 16% churn:

**LTV = $9 ÷ 16% = $56.25**

**LTV:CAC = $56.25 ÷ $18 = 3.1:1**

### Interpretation

The business remains just above the minimum **3:1 LTV:CAC threshold** under this severe retention stress case.

However, the margin for error becomes very small.

This means **post-90-day retention is a critical economic driver**. If the initiative fails to improve retention, Fable has limited room to absorb further deterioration in churn.

---

# 6. Payback reality check

### Base case

CAC:

**$18**

Monthly gross profit:

**$9**

Payback:

**$18 ÷ $9 = 2 months**

### CAC stress case

Assume CAC is **20% higher than projected**:

**$18 × 1.20 = $21.60**

Stress-case payback:

**$21.60 ÷ $9 = 2.4 months**

This remains comfortably below the business's maximum acceptable **6-month payback period**.

### Financial conclusion

The initiative can tolerate:

* **10 percentage points higher churn**, while remaining marginally above 3:1 LTV:CAC; and
* **20% higher CAC**, while remaining well within the six-month payback limit.

The greater financial risk is therefore **retention**, not acquisition cost.

---

# 7. Kill criterion

The kill criterion should reflect the actual business problem rather than simply measuring conversion.

### Primary kill criterion

> **If the target cohort does not demonstrate a measurable improvement in paid retention from day 90 to day 180 by the end of the agreed test period, and the resulting LTV:CAC falls below 3:1, we will stop scaling the initiative and reallocate the remaining investment to the highest-performing retention initiative.**

### Supporting product threshold

The initiative must also achieve:

> **At least 60% of users who identify a meaningful decline taking one recommended action within seven days.**

If this is not achieved, the first-value experience hypothesis has not been validated and should be redesigned before further investment.

### Financial guardrails

The initiative should only scale if:

* **LTV:CAC ≥3:1**
* **CAC payback ≤6 months**
* Post-90-day retention improves versus the control/baseline cohort
* The improvement is attributable to the intervention rather than simply increased short-term activity

---

# 8. Decision gates

The investment should pass three gates.

### Gate 1 — Does Fable create early value?

**Target:** ≥60% meaningful action within 7 days.

If no → redesign the first-value experience.

### Gate 2 — Does early value become durable value?

**Measure:** improvement in paid retention from day 90 to day 180.

If no → the initiative is not solving Fable's fundamental retention problem.

### Gate 3 — Is the improvement economically sustainable?

**Targets:**

* LTV:CAC ≥3:1
* CAC payback ≤6 months
* Economics remain viable under +10pp churn
* Economics remain viable under +20% CAC

If no → stop scaling and reallocate investment.

---

# 9. Investment thesis

Fable does not need to win by producing more meditation content, becoming a clinical platform, or simply adding social features.

The investment thesis is:

> **If Fable can help users take meaningful action early, then demonstrate that those actions help users manage their wellbeing beyond the initial acute phase, Fable can convert its strongest short-term value into a durable customer relationship.**

The financial objective is therefore not simply to increase engagement.

It is to **extend the period over which Fable remains valuable to its highest-value customers**, improving paid retention, LTV and ultimately the economics of the subscription business.

### Recommendation

**Proceed with the first-value experience redesign as the major controlled initiative, with further investment gated by evidence that increased early meaningful action translates into improved post-90-day retention and sustainable unit economics.**

### Planning assumptions to validate before committing the full budget

The $12 subscription, 6% churn, 75% gross margin and $18 CAC are **planning assumptions**, not established Fable actuals. They should be replaced with Fable's actual cohort and financial data before the final investment decision.
